Module 1: SAT Punctuation Rules (What the Test Actually Tests)
How to attack any punctuation question — 3-step method
1. Find the clause types. Is the text before the blank an Independent Clause (IC = full sentence) or Dependent/Fragment? Is the text after an IC, list, or modifier?
2. Apply the allowed patterns only. SAT has a closed set — if it is not on the list, it is wrong.
3. Check matching & traps. Pairs must match (comma–comma, dash–dash); never separate subject–verb or verb–object; colon never follows a verb/preposition.
1. Sentence boundaries — the #1 SAT skill
Two ICs can be joined in only 4 ways:
• IC . IC period
• IC ; IC semicolon
• IC , FANBOYS IC — comma + For / And / Nor / But / Or / Yet / So
• IC : IC colon, only when clause 2 explains/amplifies clause 1

Conjunctive adverbs (however, therefore, moreover, consequently, furthermore) are not FANBOYS: use IC ; however, IC.
Comma splice = always wrong: IC , IC ✗. Run-on IC IC ✗. Semicolon + FANBOYS (IC ; but IC) ✗. Comma + conjunctive adverb (IC , however IC) ✗.
2. Colons — strict entry condition
Before a colon must be a complete IC. After can be a list, single noun, phrase, or explaining IC: IC : list / detail / explanation.
Single dash — introducing an explanation works identically: IC — explanation.
Colon trap: never after a verb or preposition. ✗ The obstacles are: cost, rules... ✓ The obstacles are cost, rules... ✓ Three obstacles remain: cost, rules...
3. Semicolons — only 2 jobs
1) IC ; IC (no conjunction). 2) Super-separator in complex lists: Tokyo, Japan ; Paris, France ; Nairobi, Kenya. Nothing else. A semicolon can never join IC + fragment, DC + IC, or close a modifier.
4. Commas — 5 legal uses, 5 bans
Use comma for: 1) IC , FANBOYS IC 2) DC , IC (Although..., ...) 3) Intro phrase Phrase , IC 4) Nonessential pair , phrase , 5) Simple list A , B , and C (Oxford comma expected).
Never use a single comma to: 1) separate subject–verb 2) separate verb–object 3) surround that (restrictive) 4) join two verbs of one subject (surveyed... and recorded...) 5) separate comparison (better... than...).
5. Dashes & nonessential elements — matching rule
Removable info must be wrapped in matching marks: , X , or — X — or ( X ). Open dash → close dash. Open comma → close comma. Mixing (— X ,) is always wrong.
6. That / Which / Who (+ essential vs. nonessential names)
• that = restrictive, essential, no commas: fluids that support...
• which / who non-restrictive = extra info, comma before: fluids, which support...
• Title + Name without article = essential, no commas: novelist Isabel Allende spent...
• General noun + , Name , = nonessential: A researcher, Dr. Vance, published... / The astronomer, Carl Sagan, studied...
7. Apostrophes & possessive pronouns (high-yield table)
SituationFormExample
Singular noun'sresearcher's notebook

Module 2: 30 SAT-Style Practice Questions
Level 1: Foundation (1–10) · Level 2: Standard (11–20) · Level 3: Traps (21–30) — click an option, then Show answer
Question 1EASY
The James Webb Space Telescope collected data from distant planetary systems_______ it transmitted the high-resolution images back to researchers on Earth.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) systems, it
B) systems; it
C) systems it
D) systems, and, it
B — Sentence Boundary. Two independent clauses (ICs) with no conjunction. Semicolon is legal [IC; IC]. A is a comma splice, C is a run-on, D adds a stray second comma.
Question 2EASY
Before beginning the expedition into the deep Amazonian basin, the research team packed three indispensable items_______ solar-powered satellite phones, water purification tablets, and lightweight tents.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) items:
B) items;
C) items,
D) items—and
A — Colon: List. The text before the blank is a complete IC. A colon correctly introduces a list. Semicolon needs an IC after it (a list is not an IC). A comma cannot introduce a list here, and '—and' is unidiomatic.
Question 3EASY
During the archaeological excavation in Greece, the lead_______ field notebook was lost in a flash flood, forcing the team to reconstruct findings from secondary digital logs.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) researchers
B) researchers's
C) researcher's
D) researchers'
C — Singular Possessive. 'The lead researcher' is singular, so singular possessive researcher's ('s). B is never valid, D is plural possessive (more than one researcher).
Question 4EASY
Although the initial chemical reaction proceeded much slower than anticipated_______ the addition of a platinum catalyst rapidly accelerated the final compound synthesis.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) anticipated,
B) anticipated;
C) anticipated
D) anticipated:
A — Dependent + Independent. 'Although...' is a dependent clause (DC). Pattern must be [DC, IC] — comma separates introductory DC from the main clause. Semicolon/colon cannot follow a DC.
Question 5EASY
The new urban renewal proposal promised to improve public transport infrastructure_______ it failed to address the pressing issue of affordable housing for lower-income residents.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) infrastructure
B) infrastructure,
C) infrastructure; but
D) infrastructure, but
D — FANBOYS. Two ICs joined by coordinating conjunction 'but' (FANBOYS) require comma before it: [IC, but IC]. B alone is a comma splice; semicolon + FANBOYS is never used on the SAT.
Question 6EASY
The arctic fox relies on_______ dense winter coat for survival, changing color from brown in summer to white in winter for seamless camouflage against snow.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) it's
B) its
C) its'
D) their
B — Its vs. It's. Possessive pronoun needed (the coat belongs to the fox): 'its'. 'It's' = 'it is'. 'Its'' does not exist. 'Their' is plural; the fox is singular.
Question 7EASY
The conference program featured speeches on climate adaptation, renewable energy grid integration_______ and biodiversity conservation strategies across agricultural land.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) ,
B) ;
C) —
D) NO PUNCTUATION
A — Serial / Oxford Comma. Standard list of three items: A, B, and C. SAT expects the Oxford comma before 'and'. Semicolon/dash are only for complex lists or breaks, not here.
Question 8EASY
Dr. Aris Thorne_______ a noted specialist in marine ecology, delivered the opening address at the international ocean conservation summit in Lisbon.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) Thorne
B) Thorne,
C) Thorne;
D) Thorne—
B — Nonessential Opener. The phrase closes with a comma before 'delivered', so it must open with a matching comma. Pair must match: comma–comma. Dash or semicolon would mismatch.
Question 9EASY
The architectural restoration team made an unexpected discovery behind the gallery wall_______ a fully preserved 16th-century fresco depicting a celestial map.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) wall;
B) wall, and
C) wall—
D) wall
C — Single Dash = Colon. IC followed by dramatic explanation. Single dash works like a colon. Semicolon fails because 'a fully preserved fresco' is a fragment, not an IC. B adds an illogical 'and'.
Question 10EASY
Solar panels have become significantly more efficient over the last decade_______ high installation costs continue to deter many low-income households from adopting the technology.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) ; however,
B) , however,
C) ; however
D) . However
A — Conjunctive Adverb. Joining two ICs with 'however' requires [IC; however, IC]. B is a comma splice. C misses the comma after 'however'. D misses the comma ('. However high...' is wrong).
Question 11MEDIUM
In her analysis of 19th-century Gothic literature, scholar Maya Lin argues that one motif recurs throughout Mary Shelley's work_______ isolation in remote landscapes.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) work:
B) work;
C) work,
D) work
A — Colon: Explanation. IC before blank; 'isolation...' explains/amplifies it — colon is ideal. Semicolon fails (fragment after). Comma creates a splice/fragment problem. D creates a run-on of noun stuck to clause.
Question 12MEDIUM
The James Webb Space Telescope—a joint project involving NASA, the European Space Agency, and the Canadian Space Agency_______ has provided unprecedented imagery of deep space.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) Agency,
B) Agency—
C) Agency;
D) Agency
B — Matching Dashes. Parenthetical opens with em-dash, so it must close with em-dash. Mixing dash–comma or dash–semicolon is always wrong on the SAT.
Question 13MEDIUM
Renowned novelist_______ spent years studying historical archives in Mexico City before writing her award-winning book on early trade networks.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) Isabel Allende,
B) Isabel Allende—
C) , Isabel Allende,
D) Isabel Allende
D — Essential Appositive. Title + Name with no article ('Renowned novelist Isabel Allende') is essential — no commas. Adding one comma (A/B) strands the subject from its verb.
Question 14MEDIUM
After reviewing the laboratory_______ detailed reports on groundwater contamination, the regional agency ordered an immediate halt to industrial dumping.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) technicians'
B) technician's
C) technicians
D) technicians's
A — Plural Possessive. Reports belong to multiple technicians → plural possessive technicians' (s'). B is singular. C has no possession. D is never valid for regular plurals.
Question 15MEDIUM
Many migratory bird species rely on coastal wetlands for rest during seasonal travel_______ urban encroachment has destroyed over forty percent of these critical habitats.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) travel,
B) travel;
C) travel
D) travel, and,
B — Comma Splice Avoidance. Two ICs, no conjunction → semicolon. A is the classic comma-splice trap. C is a run-on. D is doubly punctuated and ungrammatical.
Question 16MEDIUM
The recent study revealed that students who participate in peer-led study groups tend to perform better on standardized exams_______ than students who study entirely in isolation.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) ,
B) ;
C) NO PUNCTUATION
D) —
C — Unnecessary Punctuation. Never separate 'exams' from its comparative 'than...' phrase. Any single mark breaks subject–comparison flow. This is the SAT 'DELETE' question type.
Question 17MEDIUM
The museum's new exhibit—which features artifacts from the Bronze Age_______ will remain open to the public until late December.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) —
B) ,
C) ;
D) NO PUNCTUATION
A — Matching Dashes. Nonessential clause opens with '—which', so it must close with '—'. Comma mismatches the opener; semicolon cannot close a dependent modifier.
Question 18MEDIUM
Linguists have long observed that languages evolve rapidly when geographically isolated cultures interact_______ new dialects frequently emerge within a few generations.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) interact,
B) interact
C) interact and
D) interact:
D — Colon: Explanation. First clause is a complete IC; second explains the result — colon fits [IC: IC-explanation]. A is a comma splice, B is a run-on, C joins two ICs with bare 'and' (needs ', and').
Question 19MEDIUM
The hydrothermal vents along the ocean floor emit mineral-rich fluids_______ support unique ecosystems entirely independent of sunlight.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) that,
B) , which
C) which,
D) that;
B — That vs. Which. Non-restrictive 'which' clause needs a comma before 'which'. 'That' clauses are restrictive and never take a comma before 'that'. C misplaces the comma.
Question 20MEDIUM
Marine biologist Elena Gomez surveyed the coral reef off the coast of Belize_______ and recorded significant coral bleaching caused by rising water temperatures.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) Belize,
B) Belize;
C) Belize
D) Belize—
C — Compound Predicate. One subject (Gomez) + two verbs (surveyed... and recorded...) = compound predicate. Never put a comma/semicolon/dash between verb phrases sharing a subject.
Question 21HARD
The ancient clay tablets discovered by archaeologists in southern Iraq_______ contain detailed logs recording grain distribution among temple workers during the Sumerian period.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) Iraq
B) Iraq,
C) Iraq;
D) Iraq—
A — Subject–Verb. Long subject ('The ancient clay tablets... Iraq') + verb 'contain'. Never separate subject from verb with a single comma/dash/semicolon.
Question 22HARD
A prominent researcher at the Institute for Advanced Study_______ published a groundbreaking paper on quantum entanglement that challenged existing models.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) Dr. Julian Vance,
B) , Dr. Julian Vance,
C) Dr. Julian Vance
D) , Dr. Julian Vance
B — Nonessential Name. 'A prominent researcher...' + name: the indefinite 'A' signals the name is extra info → needs commas both sides. A/C miss the opening comma; D misses the closing one.
Question 23HARD
Although many historians once attributed the decline of Mayan city-states solely to warfare, recent sediment samples taken from regional lakes—which show evidence of severe, multi-decade droughts_______ suggest that environmental disaster played a greater role.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) droughts,
B) droughts—
C) droughts;
D) droughts
B — Nested Parenthetical. Inner clause opens 'lakes—which...', so it must close with matching '—' before the main verb 'suggest'. Comma mismatches; semicolon cannot end a modifier.
Question 24HARD
The main obstacles facing commercial spaceflight companies are_______ high manufacturing costs, regulatory hurdles, and unpredictable atmospheric conditions during launch.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) :
B) ;
C) —
D) NO PUNCTUATION
D — Colon Trap. Never put colon/dash/semicolon directly after linking verb 'are' or a preposition. The list flows as the complement: 'are high costs...' with no mark.
Question 25HARD
The tardigrade, a microscopic organism capable of surviving extreme fluctuations, lowers_______ metabolic rate to near zero during periods of intense environmental stress.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) its
B) it's
C) their
D) its'
A — Pronoun Agreement. 'The tardigrade' is singular → 'its'. 'Their' is plural. 'It's' = 'it is'. 'Its'' never exists.
Question 26HARD
_______ the committee members disagreed sharply on budget allocations for research software, they reached a consensus regarding hardware acquisitions for the lab.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) However,
B) Although
C) Furthermore,
D) Consequently,
B — Subordinator vs. Adverb. Only 'Although' creates a dependent clause [Although..., they...]. 'However/Furthermore/Consequently,' are conjunctive adverbs — starting with them plus a comma creates a comma splice.
Question 27HARD
Exhausted by months of deep-sea data collection_______ the marine biologists finally completed their comprehensive survey of cold-water coral reefs in the North Atlantic.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) collection,
B) collection;
C) collection—
D) collection
A — Intro Modifier. Introductory participial phrase ('Exhausted...') must be set off with a comma before the main clause subject. Semicolon needs an IC before it; a participial phrase is not an IC.
Question 28HARD
The team's research paper—published in the journal Science,_______ generated significant public interest in oceanic ecosystem restoration.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) Science—
B) Science,
C) Science;
D) Science
A — Matching Dashes. Nonessential opens with dash → must close with dash. The ',_______' after Science is the closing slot. Comma mismatches the opener.
Question 29HARD
The summit invited delegates from Tokyo, Japan_______ Paris, France_______ Nairobi, Kenya_______ and Bogotá, Colombia.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) ; ; ;
B) , , ,
C) — — —
D) : : :
A — Supersemicolons. Complex list where items already contain commas (City, Country) must use semicolons as super-separators to avoid confusion. Commas alone blur item boundaries.
Question 30HARD
In their study of ancient trade, archaeologists examined ceramic vessels found off Sicily_______ analysis of organic residue inside revealed traces of olive oil, wine, and resin from the 4th century BCE.
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) Sicily,
B) Sicily
C) Sicily, and,
D) Sicily;
D — Sentence Boundary. Two ICs with no conjunction → semicolon. A is a comma splice, B is a run-on, C is doubly wrong (', and,' + splice).
